Residential Tenancies Act 1997 — Section 29C

Residential Rental Application

Part AImportant Information
For the rental provider
  • Rental auctions are prohibited under law. You may be fined if you invite prospective renters to offer more than the advertised rent.
  • You are required to include Part B – Statement of information for rental applicants in all applications for rental agreements.
  • You must complete Part C – Mandatory disclosures checklist before giving this form to an applicant. You may be fined if you fail to do this.
  • You cannot charge fees for this application.
  • The personal information provided is confidential and must only be used to assess suitability for the property.
  • Before the successful renter moves in, you must give them 2 signed copies of the Condition report. You may be fined if you fail to do this.
  • If the application is unsuccessful, this form and any copies should be destroyed.
For the applicant(s)
  • Each prospective renter should complete a copy of this application form.
  • Contact the rental provider/agent 2 business days after lodging to check if you were successful.
  • If successful, you may be required to:
    • produce ID (usually a driver's licence or passport)
    • pay one month's rent in advance
    • pay the bond amount listed on this form
    • complete a Residential tenancy agreement to secure the premises
  • Before moving in, the rental provider must give you 2 signed copies of the Condition report.
  • The rental provider's insurance policy does not cover your possessions. It is your responsibility to insure your possessions.
Part BStatement of Information for Rental Applicants
Required by lawThe following statement is required by Schedule 1 of the Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021 and cannot be altered.

Discrimination is treating, or proposing to treat, someone unfavourably because of a personal attribute, or imposing an unreasonable requirement that disadvantages persons with a personal attribute.

In Victoria it is unlawful to discriminate against someone in relation to certain personal attributes. The following are some protected attributes:

  • Age
  • Disability (including physical, sensory, intellectual disability and mental illness)
  • Employment activity
  • Gender identity & intersex status
  • Marital status or parental status
  • Physical features
  • Political or religious belief or activity
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Race
  • Lawful sexual activity or sexual orientation
  • Association with someone who has these attributes
